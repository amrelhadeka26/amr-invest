Lesson 4 · Before you invest

Understand your money before you invest it.

Most people don’t have an investing problem. They have a visibility problem. Fix that first, and investing gets a lot easier.

By Amr El Hadeka · 4 min read

Ask a room of people what they’d do with 100,000 pounds and the answers come fast: gold, a flat, the bank, a holiday. Almost nobody asks first when they’ll need that money, whether they have savings for emergencies, or whether they owe anyone. Those questions come before any investment.

Money in, money out, what’s left

Your cash flow has three parts. Money comes in: salary, freelance work, rent you collect. Money goes out, in three kinds:

  • Fixed: rent, bills, school fees. The same every month.
  • Variable: food, transport, fuel. Needed, but it moves.
  • Lifestyle: eating out, delivery, subscriptions. Optional, and usually where the leaks are.

What’s left after all three is the only money you can invest. If you don’t know that number, everything else is guesswork.

Track it for one month

You can’t fix what you can’t see. Write down every pound you spend for a month. A notebook works. So does your phone’s notes app. I use Say, an Egyptian app where you speak an expense and it logs and categorises it for you. The tool matters less than doing it every day.

Here’s a made-up example. Karim earns 30,000 pounds a month. This is where it goes:

EGP 7,500 a month

on eating out, delivery, shopping, phone and subscriptions. That’s a quarter of his income, while only 2,000 goes to savings.

Nobody needs to tell Karim to stop eating. But once he sees that number, finding an extra 1,000 pounds a month to invest stops feeling impossible. Tracking isn’t about cutting everything. It’s about choosing what your money does.

Pay yourself first

“I’ll save what’s left at the end of the month” almost never works, because nothing is left. Flip the order. On payday, move a fixed amount to savings or investments first, ideally automatically, and live on the rest. Automation removes the monthly decision, and the decision is where most people give up.

Build a safety net before you invest

Two things come before investing:

  • An emergency fund of three to six months of expenses, kept somewhere safe and easy to reach. Without it, a job loss or a hospital bill forces you to sell your investments at the worst moment, and that’s how temporary drops become real losses.
  • No expensive debt. Credit cards and consumer loans often cost more than investments are likely to earn. Paying them off is a guaranteed return.

Inflation is why this matters

13.9%

Egypt’s urban inflation in September 2026. At that pace, prices double in about five years.

Money sitting still loses buying power every year. The rate changes, but it rarely stops. That’s the reason to invest at all. The foundations above are what let you invest calmly instead of in a panic.

Give your money a goal

“I want to be rich” is a wish. A goal answers three questions: what, how much, and by when. For example: a 300,000-pound flat down payment in five years.

With a five-year goal, a fair share should still stay in safer places, especially as the date gets closer. But once the goal has a number and a date, every other decision gets simpler.

Before you invest your money, understand where it’s going, protect it, and give it a job.
