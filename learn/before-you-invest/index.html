Your money first · Before you invest

Understand your money before you invest it.

Most people don’t have an investing problem. They have a visibility problem. Fix that first, and investing gets a lot easier.

By Amr El Hadeka · 3 min read

In 30 seconds

  • Find out what’s left each month. That’s the only money you can invest.
  • Track every pound for one month.
  • Build an emergency fund and clear expensive debt first.

Ask people what they’d do with 100,000 pounds and the answers come fast: gold, a flat, the bank. Almost nobody first asks when they’ll need it, or whether they have savings for emergencies.

Money in, money out, what’s left

Money goes out three ways: fixed (rent, bills, school fees), variable (food, transport) and lifestyle (eating out, delivery, subscriptions), which is usually where the leaks are. What’s left is the only money you can invest.

Track it for one month

Write down every pound for a month. A notebook works. I use Say, an Egyptian app where you speak an expense and it logs it for you. Here’s a made-up example: Karim earns 30,000 pounds a month.

Lifestyle takes 7,500 pounds a month, a quarter of his income, while only 2,000 goes to savings. Once he sees that, finding an extra 1,000 to invest stops feeling impossible.

Pay yourself first

“I’ll save what’s left” rarely works, because nothing is left. On payday, move a fixed amount to savings or investments first, ideally automatically, and live on the rest.

Two things before investing

  • An emergency fund of three to six months of expenses, safe and easy to reach, so a job loss or a hospital bill doesn’t force you to sell at the worst moment.
  • No expensive debt. Credit cards and consumer loans often cost more than investments earn. Paying them off is a guaranteed return.

Then give your money a goal: what, how much, by when. For example, a 300,000-pound down payment in five years.

Before you invest your money, understand where it’s going, protect it, and give it a job.
