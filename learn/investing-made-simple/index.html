How investing works · Investing made simple

Investing is putting your money to work.

Five ideas cover most of what a beginner needs. The goal isn’t to get rich fast. It’s to not get poorer, slowly.

By Amr El Hadeka · 2 min read

In 30 seconds

  • Money can work three ways: lend it, own a business, or own a thing.
  • Save what you need soon. Invest what can wait three years or more.
  • Judge any return after inflation, not before.

Three ways money can work

  • Lend it and earn interest: certificates, T-bills, bonds.
  • Own a share of a business and share its profits: stocks.
  • Own a thing that can rise in value or pay rent: gold, property.

Everything else, from funds to property apps, is one of these or a mix.

Saving protects. Investing grows.

Money you need within three years, and your emergency fund, stays saved: steady and easy to reach. Money that can wait longer can be invested. It will go up and down, but it aims to beat inflation. You need both.

More return means more bumps

There’s no higher return without higher risk. The real question is whether you can sit through a drop without selling. And if anyone offers a “high, guaranteed return,” walk away.

Time does the heavy lifting

Your returns start earning returns of their own. The earlier you start, the bigger that effect. See it in the next lesson ›

Real return: what’s left after inflation

About 4.5% real

A deposit at the Central Bank’s 19% overnight rate, against 13.9% inflation (Sept 2026). Exactly: 1.19 ÷ 1.139 − 1.

Always ask what something pays after inflation. For years, Egyptian savers lost money while thinking they were earning.

Investing isn’t about getting rich quickly. It’s about making informed choices and giving your money time to grow.
