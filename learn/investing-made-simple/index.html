Lesson 5 · Investing made simple

Investing is putting your money to work.

Five ideas cover most of what a beginner needs. The goal isn’t to get rich fast. It’s to not get poorer, slowly.

By Amr El Hadeka · 4 min read

Investing sounds complicated because of the words around it. Underneath, there are only three ways to put money to work, and five ideas that explain how it behaves.

Three ways money can work

  • Lend it. To a government, a company or a bank, and get paid interest. Certificates, T-bills and bonds work this way.
  • Own a share of a business. When the business grows and earns profits, you share in them. That’s what stocks are.
  • Own a thing. Gold or property, whose value can rise, or which pays you rent.

Everything you’ll come across, from funds to fractional property apps, is one of these, or a mix.

Saving protects. Investing grows.

Saving is for money you’ll need within one to three years, and for your emergency fund. It’s steady and easy to reach. Investing is for money you won’t need for three years or more. You’ll see ups and downs along the way, but it aims to beat inflation over time. You need both. They just have different jobs.

More return usually means more bumps

This is the one rule that never breaks: there’s no higher return without higher risk. A certificate moves slowly and steadily. Stocks can go further, but along the way there will be stretches where your money is worth less than you put in. The real question isn’t which is better. It’s whether you can sit through the drops without selling, and when you’ll need the money.

If anyone offers you a “high, guaranteed return,” something is wrong.

Time does the heavy lifting

Compounding means your returns start earning returns of their own. Its key ingredient is time. Say you start with 10,000 pounds and add 1,000 a month, at an assumed 10% a year:

In the last ten years alone, growth adds more than everything you paid in over all thirty.

Real markets don’t rise 10% every year like a ruler. Some years are up, some are down. But the idea holds: whoever starts early has time working for them. Try your own numbers in grow.

When do you need it back?

Before choosing anything, ask how long the money can stay invested. Six months: keep it stable and reachable. Five years: balance growth with safety. Twenty years: you have time to ride out the downs.

Ask about liquidity too: how fast you can turn something into cash without losing value. A flat can take months to sell. A certificate can be broken, often with a penalty. A fund in an app usually takes days.

Real return = return minus inflation

The return you hear about isn’t what you really gain. Take out inflation first.

About 4.5% real

A deposit at the Central Bank’s 19% overnight rate, against 13.9% inflation (Sept 2026). Exactly: 1.19 ÷ 1.139 − 1.

Right now, that’s positive, which is good news for savers. In past years, rates sat well below inflation, and people lost money while thinking they were earning. So always ask: what does it pay after inflation? And remember that steady, regular investing usually beats chasing whatever pays the most this month.

Investing isn’t about getting rich quickly. It’s about making informed choices and giving your money time to grow.
