Build your plan · Your investment mindset

Every asset has its bad year.

That’s why you mix them. Successful investing is as much about your behaviour and your mix as about what you buy.

By Amr El Hadeka · 3 min read

In 30 seconds

  • No asset wins every year. That’s why you mix them.
  • Invest at the lower of the risk you can stomach and the risk you can afford.
  • Invest the same amount every month, and rebalance once or twice a year.

Here are four made-up years and how four kinds of assets might move in each, in Egyptian pounds. The numbers are assumptions, not forecasts.

No single asset wins all four. Diversification won’t stop every loss, but it stops one bad year from sinking everything. Across these same four years, a cautious mix never had a losing year, while all-in-stocks ranged from −30% to +15%.

How you feel vs. what you can afford

Risk tolerance is your feelings: would you sell after a 15% drop? Risk capacity is your facts: stable income, an emergency fund, who depends on you, and when you need the money. Invest at the lower of the two.

Same amount, every month

Investing a fixed amount on a schedule means you never have to guess the right moment, and you keep buying when everyone else is scared. In a market that dips and recovers, it can beat investing all at once; in one that only rises, it lags. Its real value is behavioural.

Your feelings are expensive

FOMO buys near the top. Panic sells near the bottom. The fix is a written plan made while you’re calm, and automatic monthly investing.

Check in, don’t obsess

After a strong year your mix drifts, and you carry more risk than you chose. Once or twice a year, bring it back: direct new money to what shrank, or sell a little of what grew.

What you invest in matters. How you behave when it falls matters just as much.
