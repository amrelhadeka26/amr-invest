Lesson 10 · Your first steps

Your first week as an investor.

You don’t need to know everything to start. You need the basics, good questions, and one small step this week.

By Amr El Hadeka · 4 min read

If you’ve read the lessons before this one, you already know more than most people who invest. The gap now isn’t knowledge. It’s action. This lesson pulls everything together into a path you can follow.

Six ideas to take with you

  1. Know where your money goes before you invest it.
  2. Time, consistency and low fees do the heavy lifting.
  3. Every asset has a job, and a bad year.
  4. Ask five questions before any app: licence, what you own, cost, risk, exit.
  5. Mix assets to fit your goal, your time horizon and your capacity for risk.
  6. Your behaviour matters as much as your picks.

The 10-step roadmap

Notice that investing itself comes late. The first half is all about your own money.

  1. Track your expenses.
  2. Understand your cash flow: what comes in, what goes out, what’s left.
  3. Build an emergency fund of three to six months.
  4. Pay off expensive debt.
  5. Define your goals: what, how much, by when.
  6. Know how much risk you can carry, in feelings and in facts.
  7. Learn the main asset classes.
  8. Research platforms and products with the five questions.
  9. Write your plan down.
  10. Review it once or twice a year.

Your first week

Reading is easy to forget. Doing isn’t. Four things for the next seven days:

1 · Track every pound

For seven days. A notebook, your notes app, or a tracker like Say.

2 · Write one goal

What, how much, and by when. One sentence.

3 · Count your safety net

How many months of expenses could you cover today?

4 · Open one fund page

In any app. Answer the five questions about it. Don’t buy anything yet.

When you’ve done all four, write to me at invest@amrelhadeka.com and tell me what surprised you. I read every message.

Quick check

You need the money in six months. Stocks or savings?

Savings. Six months is too short to sit through a stock market drop.

True or false: a diversified portfolio can’t lose money.

False. Diversification reduces the damage from any single asset, but when many assets fall together, a mixed portfolio falls too.

What comes first: investing, or an emergency fund?

The emergency fund. Without it, an unexpected bill can force you to sell at the worst time.

Understand the fundamentals, ask the right questions, then decide for yourself. That’s what being an investor means.
