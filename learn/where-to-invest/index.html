Lesson 7 · Where can you invest?

Every investment has a job, and a risk.

Eight places your money can go. Understanding the difference between them matters more than chasing the highest return.

By Amr El Hadeka · 4 min read

I won’t tell you what to buy. What I can do is show you what each option is, how it earns, and what can go wrong, so you can ask better questions about anything you’re offered.

Lending: cash and fixed income

Cash means savings accounts, certificates and deposits. It earns interest, is easy to reach, and is the right home for your emergency fund. The catch: penalties for breaking certificates early, and over long periods it can trail inflation.

Fixed income means lending to the government or companies: T-bills for three to twelve months, bonds for longer, and sukuk as the Sharia-compliant option. Money market and fixed-income funds hold a basket of these for you. Risk is low but not zero: when rates change, fund prices can dip.

Owning: stocks and index funds

Stocks make you part-owner of a company, on the Egyptian Exchange or abroad. You earn when the share price grows, and from dividends if the company pays them. Prices swing hard, and a single company can fail. An equity fund spreads your money across many companies.

Index funds take that further: instead of trying to pick winners, you buy a whole market in one fund. An ETF trades during the day like a stock; a mutual fund is priced once a day. One fact matters more than most beginners realise:

Owning things: gold and property

Gold earns only through its price. It has protected many Egyptians when the pound weakened, but it pays no income and can go flat or fall for years. Physical gold carries making charges and storage; gold funds and digital gold let you start small, with buy and sell fees. Gold preserves. It doesn’t multiply.

Real estate pays through rent and price growth. A whole flat is a big ticket, slow to sell, and has upkeep. Fractional apps let you own a share of a unit from a few thousand pounds; check carefully how you get out. REITs are listed companies that own property and trade like stocks.

Beyond borders, and beyond risk

International investing spreads your money across other economies and currencies. The pound went from around 8 to over 50 per dollar in about a decade, which is why many Egyptians want some dollar exposure. Currency moves both ways, though, and transfers come with limits and fees. Thndr now gives Egyptian residents access to US stocks.

Crypto is speculative. Bitcoin has fallen more than 70% several times. Investor protection is thin, and in Egypt, issuing, trading or promoting crypto requires a Central Bank licence. If you go near it, use only money you could lose entirely.

Match the tool to the goal

Try this with your own goals:

  • Needed in six months? Safety and access matter most. Cash or money market funds.
  • A home in five years? A balance: some growth, a solid safe portion, and more safety as the date gets close.
  • Wealth over twenty years? You can sit through ups and downs, so growth assets and low fees matter most.

These are ways of thinking, not instructions. Your answer depends on your situation.

Different investments do different jobs. Know the job, the risk and the cost before the return.
