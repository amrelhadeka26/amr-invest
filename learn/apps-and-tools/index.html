Getting started · Apps and tools

Five questions before any investing app.

Apps have made investing easier than ever in Egypt. Understanding the product and its risks still comes first.

By Amr El Hadeka · 3 min read

In 30 seconds

  • Ask five questions before any app: licence, what you own, cost, risk, exit.
  • Most apps start you with funds: fixed income, equity or gold.
  • Walk away from anything “guaranteed” or unlicensed.

Apps have made investing in Egypt easier than ever. Platforms change their products and fees often, so the most useful thing I can give you is a way to judge any of them. Apps mentioned here are things to research, not recommendations.

Three fund types, three jobs

  • Fixed income fund: T-bills, bonds and deposits. For stability.
  • Equity fund: shares in many companies. For growth, with bad years.
  • Gold fund: gold without the storage. For protection, with no income.

Thndr, and Thndr Alpha

Thndr is an FRA-licensed Egyptian app with funds, Egyptian stocks, and US stocks for Egyptian residents. Its tool Alpha asks three questions about your goal, how you handle ups and downs, and when you’ll need the money, then suggests a starting mix of funds, with a Sharia-compliant option. Treat it as a starting point, not a decision.

Property in shares, and moving money

In Egypt, Nawy Shares (from about 20,000 pounds) and SAFE by Madinet Masr (from 5,000 pounds) sell shares in property. In Dubai, Stake and SmartCrowd are regulated by the DFSA. Rental income isn’t guaranteed, so always check how you get out. Nsave offers a USD account: it moves money, it doesn’t invest it.

Walk away if you hear

  • “Guaranteed high returns.”
  • “Decide today, spots are limited.”
  • No licence you can look up yourself, or no clear way to get your money out.

Apps make investing easier. They don’t make it risk-free. Ask the five questions every time.
