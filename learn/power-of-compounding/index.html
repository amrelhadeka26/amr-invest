How investing works · Compounding

Compounding: let your money make money.

Returns that earn their own returns. It starts slowly, then speeds up, and its most important ingredient is time.

By Amr El Hadeka · 2 min read

In 30 seconds

  • Your returns start earning returns of their own.
  • It feels slow at first, then speeds up. Time is the key ingredient.
  • It works against you too: inflation, debt and fees compound the same way.

If you remember one idea from these lessons, make it this one.

Interest on interest

Put 10,000 pounds somewhere that earns 10% a year. Year one earns 1,000. Year two earns 1,100, because 11,000 is now working. Leave it 30 years, add nothing, and it becomes about 174,000.

Starting early beats starting big

Sara invests 500 pounds a month from 25 to 55. Omar invests 2,000 a month from 40 to 55. At an assumed 10% a year, Sara ends with about 1.03 million and Omar with about 797,000. She put in half as much. Her secret was fifteen extra years.

The rule of 72

Divide 72 by a yearly rate to see roughly how many years it takes to double. At 10%, money doubles in about 7 years. At 13.9% inflation, prices double in about 5. At 30% on a credit card, debt doubles in under 2.5.

It works against you too

Inflation compounds on cash that sits still. Debt compounds on unpaid balances. Fees compound on everything you invest. So clear expensive debt, keep fees low, and aim for a return above inflation. Try your own numbers in grow.

The best day to start compounding was years ago. The second best is this month, with whatever amount you have.
